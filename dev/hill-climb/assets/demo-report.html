Why this method

Targets and feedback. Models improve reliably when every round returns a score and reasons. A target such as “every judge scores at least 9” gives the builder something to climb; the judges’ notes tell it which way is up.

Not bottlenecked on articulation. Hand-written principles cap the result at what someone managed to write down (the bitter lesson). Independent judges with broad taste score the work and the builder searches for improvements, including ones nobody would have specified.

Human signal still counts. The owner’s preferences, examples, reference products, corrections and vetoes enter as rubric criteria, anchors, comparators and hard checks, listed below. The owner’s own look is the final gate; model agreement is not.

Read scores as signal, not truth. The same judge can score the same thing differently on another run; comparators drift as the candidate improves. Judges did not see the target unless the header says so.
