# Medium Guide

Read the relevant section after `principles.md`. Apply the shared decisions to the delivery context; do not carry one medium's constraints into another.

## Slides

Distinguish presenter-led slides from a deck read independently. Speech can carry explanation in the first; a self-paced deck needs enough visible text or an accessible companion to stand alone.

- Plan a coherent progression around the learner's question. Use an overview when the audience needs orientation; omit it for a brief, already situated explanation.
- Keep related diagrams, examples and comparisons available together. Split a crowded slide when it asks learners to process unrelated ideas, not to enforce one bullet per slide.
- Reveal steps in a process when that supports the explanation. After a reveal, hold the relevant state; preserve earlier states when comparison matters. A reveal is not a reason for continuous background movement.
- Put labels next to the part being discussed. Remove text only if its explanatory role is supplied elsewhere in that delivery mode.
- Provide a readable order and meaningful descriptions in the intended export. Check projected size as well as the reading version; do not assume speaker notes reach the learner.

Plan/output: learning sequence, slide purpose, layout and attention notes; speaker cues only when relevant/requested; editable deck or source model and requested exports. Review both live builds and the final static/readable form when both will be used.

## Static Graphics and Diagrams

Design for self-paced inspection without assuming narration, hover or animation.

- Choose the representation for the relation: spatial structure, flow, comparison, chronology or quantitative change. A node graph is not the default for every topic.
- Provide a useful entry point, nearby labels and enough explanation to interpret the graphic. Use a reading path when order matters; allow direct inspection when it does not.
- Label ambiguous relationships. Make arrow direction and meaning consistent; distinguish time order, flow and causation. Obvious spatial containment may need no repeated verbal label.
- Show change using ordered snapshots, small multiples or before/after views when needed. State what changes and what stays fixed.
- Preserve scales, units, categories, denominators, important exceptions and source context. Explain a schematic abstraction or model assumption where readers could take it literally.
- Provide a text equivalent that conveys the key relation, not merely the appearance. For data-rich figures, include accessible data or a structured description as appropriate. Check colour-independent meaning and readability at intended size.

Plan/output: purpose, layout, labels and relationship meanings, explanatory text, evidence and access notes; editable source plus requested image/vector/document export. No narration, timings, chapters or controls unless independently required.

## Interactive Views and Visualisations

Justify each interaction by what it lets the learner inspect, compare, predict or test. Do not add controls merely to make a static explanation feel interactive.

- Establish an intelligible initial state. For a complex unfamiliar system, an overview or guided example may help before free exploration. Experts may need direct filtering or lookup instead.
- Keep the current selection, changed variable, units and result visible. Explain feedback near the change. Preserve a baseline for comparisons where memory alone would be unreliable.
- Separate actions that inspect an explanation from actions that leave it. For a research map, selecting a node can open a local explanation of the claim and its relevance, with a separately labelled source link. For a source directory or an explicit lookup task, direct navigation may be the correct primary action.
- Make connector semantics and source/interpretation boundaries legible. Do not turn proximity into an evidential claim or present a model output as an observation.
- Offer meaningful reset/back/step/replay controls when the interaction needs them. When teaching an irreversible event, such as information sent externally, keep its history visible through back/route changes; label reset as a fresh simulation rather than undoing the event. Do not force auto-play or linear progression when learner inspection is the purpose.
- Provide keyboard operation, visible focus, accessible names and a way to obtain essential information without hover. Keep focus predictable when panels update. Offer an equivalent explanation for a graph or canvas that assistive technology cannot interpret.
- Respect reduced-motion needs without losing causal or temporal information; step-through states or a stable comparison may substitute for animation.

Plan/output: initial state, learner actions, state changes, explanatory feedback, source access and recovery, access alternatives; editable content/data and interface source plus requested output. Review actual action-result pairs, reset/back, keyboard use and reduced-motion behaviour. An attractive screenshot does not validate interaction.

## Video and Animation

Use the shared principles to decide what changes, how attention follows it and where learners need a stable inspection state.

- Align explanation with the visible event; preserve important before/after states when learners need comparison.
- Dense unfamiliar content often needs stillness. Modest highlights, changing phrases or transitions may guide attention in an overview without depicting a physical process.
- Distinguish designed display text from accessibility captions. Provide captions, transcript and essential visual description as appropriate.
- Match pacing and learner control to the subject, audience and platform. No fixed ideal duration or compulsory continuous motion.

Plan/output here: the explanatory sequence, motion/attention decisions, inspection states, access and review criteria. For recording, segment-level storyboards, authoring formats and rendering, use `designing-instructional-videos` if available, or the chosen production tool's documentation. This shared skill does not depend on either.
