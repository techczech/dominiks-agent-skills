# Purpose, reader and genre

Write a declaration for the whole work before its outline and for every section before its draft. Start from the author's current words and the reader's task. For a new piece or changed direction, establish the author's position through [the author interview](author-interview.md) before filling the declaration. Link confirmed answers and writing decisions; label proposed interpretations and unresolved assumptions. A declaration makes the intended explanation inspectable; it is not text to paste into the finished work.

## Declaration fields

Save declarations as readable Markdown. Group related fields under headings; use bullets for reader knowledge, exclusions and required outputs, numbered lists for sequences, and tables when comparing fields. Keep each field visually separate. Do not concatenate the declaration into one paragraph or a semicolon-separated string. Structured agent records must also be readable by the author; an agent-facing register does not remove this requirement.

| Field | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | What this work or section must establish, explain or enable. |
| Author position | The author's claim, intended emphasis and reasoning. Identify exact wording to retain and deleted material to keep out. |
| Reader | Who will read it, including relevant role, experience and domain knowledge. |
| Reason for reading | The question, decision or activity that brings that reader to the text. |
| Prior knowledge | Concepts, facts and procedures the reader can reasonably be expected to know. Separate an evidenced audience assumption from a guess. |
| Required knowledge or action | What the reader must understand or be able to do after reading. Name the actual knowledge, not a feeling of confidence or importance. |
| Irrelevant material | Topics, history, technical detail or commentary that do not serve this purpose for this reader. |
| Genre and destination | Form, venue or commission; required parts, conventions, length and acceptable narrative freedom. |
| Scope and authority | Requested work, protected author text, decisions already authorised and decisions still awaiting the author. |
| Source and state | Current source files and passage locators, declaration version, status and approval record. |

Apply the same declaration to an explanatory visual, table, appendix or reusable resource. State the local reader question, the relationship or action the artefact communicates, prerequisite knowledge and irrelevant content; link the source claims/data and its place in the narrative.

A section declaration inherits the whole-work declaration by link, then states the local reader question, knowledge before/after, opening proposition and boundaries. It must add concrete local content. Repeating the whole-work declaration does not explain the section's job.

The section opens with a clear statement of its purpose. Use a substantive proposition that identifies what the section establishes or explains; do not add a stock introductory sentence that says only that the topic is important.

## When a paragraph or sentence is doubtful

Add a local entry to the section brief or critique:

- Locator and exact passage.
- Reader question at this point.
- Knowledge already established.
- New claim, reasoning, definition, instruction or example supplied.
- Connection to the section purpose and next step.
- Decision: keep, delete or rewrite; reason and source.

If the passage supplies no needed content, delete it within the authorised scope. Do not preserve it through a different connector or a generic qualification. If it carries necessary content in the wrong place, record where it belongs. Keep author text protected by the request; identify an out-of-scope concern separately.

Do not create a separate file for every obvious sentence. The section record can hold local decisions. Split a record when a particular issue requires independent discussion or evidence.

## Genre constrains the explanation

Record the actual target instructions before choosing the structure. The following are starting distinctions, not substitutes for venue requirements.

| Genre | Structural requirements and narrative choices |
| --- | --- |
| Empirical medical or scientific paper | Preserve the required methods, results and discussion functions and the venue's specified headings. Plan how the argument develops within those parts. Methods identify what was done; discussion relates observations to the research question. |
| Humanities paper | Organise the argument around the problem, interpretation, evidence and reasoning. A separate experimental-methods section is not a default requirement. Follow the relevant discipline and venue. |
| Report | Supply an executive summary when the commission calls for one. Arrange the body around the decisions and knowledge the readers need. The body can use a different order from an academic paper. |
| Essay or Substack post | Choose the order that develops the author's argument and the reader's understanding. A scene, example, claim or question may open it when supplied by the author. Do not invent one to satisfy a genre template. |

Distinguish genre conventions from voice and typography. A required discussion section does not require bureaucratic sentences. Freedom of structure does not authorise invented claims or personal asides.

## Read and update

Each writer and critic opens the current declaration file and its named author sources. Record the exact versions read in that stage's record. If the author changes the purpose, reader, claim or exclusions, mark dependent briefs and drafts for recheck. Do not silently treat an older declaration as current.

## Check the declaration before handoff

Check that each required field contains actual content or an explicit source locator. A section may inherit entry/exit knowledge or author wording from another current file, but its brief must name that file and passage. Every protected source that the next agent may use belongs in the required-input list. Map shared author claims to this section or to an earlier established claim; a section need not repeat the whole argument.
