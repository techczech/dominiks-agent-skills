# Rules

Sections follow the 12 steps in `SKILL.md`. Ids are stable aliases for older citations: `N1-N91` (review notes; the second N87 is now N91), `1.1-10.1` (original sections), `D1-D19` (13 Nov decisions, stated in full below).

- Newest wins. A brief, recipe or earlier build never overrides a rule here (N75).
- Enforcement tags: `[kit]` the kit element draws it right by default; `[lint]` `kit/tools/lint-frames.mjs`; `[pre]` `node tools/prerelease.mjs .`; `[look]` only the stills look catches it.
- A rule that held for one video belongs in that video's notes, not here (his test, 13 Nov). New comments go to `feedback-ledger.md` (optional local log of the user's comments, kept beside the skill or in the project and created on first use; never part of the published skill) the same day (D15).
- Quotes are Dominik's, shortened.

## Step 1: brief (genre, audience, mode)

- **N31 Genre first.** Name genre and audience before planning. Genres: instruction; illustrated orientation (understand and want something: "illustrating, getting people excited"); talk summary (N76); story with characters (N81); silent overview (D16). Orientation is not promotion: no slogans or calls to action, but it may be lively and carries the speaker's view.
- **N45 Two modes, recorded as `mode:` in the outline frontmatter.** `explain`: beginners meeting a hard idea; one concept per screen, few elements, `calm`, worked examples, longer holds. `navigate`: a guided tour of a paper, report or talk; more beats, a visual per point (N48), a new screen per elaboration (N49), `standard`, concrete cases from the source (N51). Paper, report and talk-summary videos default to `navigate`.
- **1.2, 8.1, 8.2, 8.5, N69 Motion level.** `calm`: fade only, no travel, no highlight, no count-up; for `explain`, definitions, `code` and `table`, beginners. `standard`: fade plus at most 28 px travel and a highlight on the item spoken (D9); for `navigate`, orientation, recaps. Audience unstated: `standard`, and say so; a talk teaching a hard new concept needs `calm`, so ask. The hold-still rule (step 9, long dwells) applies to `calm` only; `navigate` follows the N69 cadence.
- **N76 A talk summary is a new script in the house voice, not a cut of clips.** "Take the transcript of what I said and make a more coherent story" (4 Nov). Route A: one connected script from transcript, Notes and slides; about 5-7 minutes for an hour's lecture, or several 2-4 minute videos (one per topic or activity) for a workshop day. The recording is the evidence, not the soundtrack. His own voice cut into in-order excerpts (route B) only when he asks.
- **N81 Story with characters.** Told as a story (a meeting, a difference, a turn), no part labels. Each character has a designed voice fitting the brief (ask when it gives no age or gender coding), written and voiced as a conversation. Characters move as the source art draws them; props look like the originals. Music only when he asks (`voice.md`).
- **D16 Silent video (explainer top).** About 30 s, a quick overview, fully descriptive and visual. Opens by saying what it is about, then lists its steps (`steps`: the claim in a left column, the numbered rows with pictures on the right, the default when a lead is given; Dominik 13 Nov "claim left and list right"); step cards with large text (D6); one uncluttered example screen per step; enough motion that the point lands at once. An end card naming the site is allowed only here. After the opening it lists the steps it will show: a `steps` screen (lead line, up to six numbered rows with thumbnails) `[kit]`.
- **Ending.** A voiced video ends on one closing sentence that states the answer; no call to action (3.4).

## Step 2: evidence and privacy

- **Evidence.** The talk, outline and speaker's own examples are the evidence. For an outline-only video the outline and its narration are the evidence, and "speaker" rules apply to the narration.
- **3.1-3.4, N4 Additions.** Add only what the speaker says aloud and the screen cannot show alone. An addition is a scene of its own (an interlude: `inserted: true`, crossfade in), uses the speaker's words, covers no slide text, and is logged in the report with quote and time. Never add facts, numbers, names, stock footage, sound effects, lower-thirds, slogans, calls to action, decorative shapes, or a "better" diagram of what was said. Allowed: line art of what is described (N36), the talk's widgets and charts (N39), official logos (N38). Music: only N81.
- **3.5, N79 Numbers and labels follow the picture and the slide.** A number, label or name on screen matches the image or chart it sits on, and the narration says the same. Narration we write: where they differ the picture or slide wins and the script changes; where the transcript is garbled go by the slides. Figures come from the artefact shown, not a number remembered aloud. Route B (his recording): show the outline and report the difference; correct neither silently. List every change in the report.
- **N78 Privacy pass on every picture, before voicing and again before anything is published.** "Redraw images that have [private group] or any personal project identifying information." Check every still and capture, his own slides included, for: email addresses; order and booking references; forms; private project, group and repository names (sidebars, window titles, tabs); home-folder paths; machine, library and account names; attendee lists naming people or groups; faces. Redraw as generic line art, or recapture cropped or masked (not cropped so it hides his request). List every redraw and mask in the report. `[pre]` checks narration and frame text against `prerelease-deny.txt`; pictures are `[look]`.
- **N80 How-to content shows real captures in order.** "Show people screenshots of exactly what it looks like." Main pictures are real captures of the actual states (prompt, approval dialog, file written, before and after), cropped large (N61); a prepared example is labelled as prepared. Line art is for ideas with no real screen. Narrows N33 and N36 for how-to; N66 still governs evidence pages.
- **N37 Processes as ordinary actions.** Show the steps a person would recognise (type a query, open a drawer, read a file), then the same steps by the agent as visible tool calls. No terminal unless the audience is technical. Examples for general points are ordinary knowledge-worker tasks.
- **N52 Show where it comes from.** A paper or report explainer shows the source itself (title page, journal, authors and year legible) and the page, figure or table it draws on, with a small page chip. A paywalled or copyrighted page is fine for internal teaching; mark the video not cleared for public hosting.
- **N53 Mine existing explainers.** When a guide, visual guide or widget set exists for the same source, take scenes from its panels and widgets (N39) before inventing pictures; list which.
- **N39, D11 Reuse the source's working widgets and mock chats.** Show them through a `widget` scene (`?video=1`, N60), 1920x1080, built in step with speech, instead of redrawing. A slide that is only a live embed is captured, not skipped.
- **N26, N38, N62, N89 Logos.** Every named product or company carries its official mark, never redrawn. Lookup order: TalkWeaver's sets (`look.json` `iconSets` or `TALKWEAVER_ICONS`); then the product's own site or repository, saved under `assets/logos/` with its source URL in the README; a name tile only when no official mark exists, and say so. Only a mark whose every painted fill and stroke is white is recoloured to ink; two-colour marks keep their colours (`_icons.mjs` `lightModeFix`). A solid box where a mark should be is a bug `[look]`.
- **N38 Progress over time is a real chart** from `ai-model-benchmarks` data, not a list.
- **N82 Transparent PNG or WebP is flattened onto white** (or the frame's card colour) when converted; look for black or missing areas.

## Step 3: narrative outline

- **N40 Narrative before script, script before pictures.** Save the audience's starting knowledge, the claims to establish and a narrative outline as files; review; then one connected section's spoken draft together with its visual states; review; then the next. Per part record what the viewer knows on entry, the question answered, and what they understand on exit. Voice and render only after the whole script and storyboard are reviewed.
- **N50 Introduction and narrated links.** Open with an introduction saying where the video goes. The first sentence is a claim about the subject, then names the source: "the start should be a statement" (6 Nov). Never open on "This video explains" `[pre]`. Each section starts with a bridge sentence linking the last point to the next. Split a difficult concept over several screens, each with a concrete example.
- **N57 Bridges are conversational and use verbs.** Over a section card the voice bridges as a presenter would; it never reads out or paraphrases the card title, and uses verbs ("To close that gap, the paper proposes...") not noun labels.
- **N55, N56 Section card titles are the chapters.** A 4-9 word lead-in built on a verb or question word ("What problem the paper sets out to solve"), never a single noun or a cute label. They become `chapters.txt` and the companion's table of contents; write them for someone scanning a list.

## Step 4: narration script

- **N64 A person explaining.** Continuous prose in full sentences with explicit links (why the next point matters, what it answers, how it follows or contrasts). Read across scene boundaries it sounds like one person. No fragments, slogans or promo cadence, even when the screens are short. Write with `expository-writing`, then `cohesion-review`, then `unslop` (N42); read the whole script aloud in order before voicing. Tighten by cutting points, never connectives.
- **N41 Narrate the development, not the numbering.** No "step one, step two", no category labels read aloud `[pre]`; say what changed and why. Step cards (D6) are a picture device, not narration.
- **N42 No hedges, no "what it does not mean", no AI patterns.** Plain claims; errors in ordinary words ("they can make errors, but similar to human errors"); no explaining of dips or exceptions the audience does not need `[pre]`.
- **N74 Name the speaker once.** Full name at first mention, then the first name or "he" `[pre]`.
- **N34, 4.1 Voice and picture have different jobs.** The picture shows the thing at a scale that fills the frame; the voice says what it means. Words the picture shows need not be spoken. Narration does not read bullets back; in an outline the narration is the learning content and the bullets are changing phrases.
- Pace and the rest of the voice: `voice.md` (N67).

## Steps 5 and 6: storyboard and approval

### What is shown

- **N33 Everything is illustrated.** "The slides are the key points but they need something else to support them in video." Each spoken idea gets a picture of the thing itself: a person doing the action, the object, the real interface state, the talk's chart. Text-only templates (`list`, `statement`, `cards`) are for genuinely verbal content. Exception, D5: when the thing said is a name, date or plain claim, large text is its picture.
- **N36 Line-art system.** Ink outlines on paper, one accent (kit green), red only for errors. One recurring main character with the same face in every frame, a few secondary characters, varied extras. An agent is a chat window with an "Agent" tab and visible tool calls (a chip naming the tool; dashed arrow only for a tool call); no robots, no faces on AI. What the model sees is a tray ("Model's context") that fills only with what tools return. Format: `inputs.md`, "Frame JSON".
- **N43 A metaphor is drawn as the real thing behaves** (a ladder is climbed upwards); level names come from the speaker, verbatim.
- **N51 The picture may elaborate, not echo:** an example, case, figure or table row from the source. For a paper prefer the concrete systems and results over the abstract framework: name the system, show what it did, cite the page.
- **N68 Every sentence is supported; a problem is shown as a problem.** Each claim-adding clause gets an on-screen action at its words. When the narration says something fails, struggles or is missing, the picture shows the failure (the thing failing, a red mark, a blocked path, the missing piece) and the reason where given; at minimum the word appears as a label.
- **N61, N66 Screenshots large and brief.** An evidence page or table fills most of the frame, stays up for one sentence (4-8 s), and the camera zooms into the region spoken about (scale-and-pan, 1.2-1.6 s). A slow zoom is not an action.
- **N60 Widgets in video mode.** `?video=1`: figure only, scaled to fill the frame; no header, step buttons, Notes, About, Reset or caption. The full interface is the self-study mode. A widget without a video mode gets one (`talkweaver-visuals`).
- **N32 Never an empty or waiting screen while someone speaks.** "When you land on a blank page... there should be something arriving." Every scene lands with its main visual arriving; no more than about 1 s with only a title or one short line while the voice runs. A sparse bullet scene is a failure, not calm design `[pre]`.
- **N49, N58 One screen, one point, one elaboration.** When the narration turns to a second element (another failure, example, stage) move to a fresh screen and build again; two pictures making two points are two screens, or one replaces the other with `hide`. Cap: about five built elements and one main focus `[lint]`. Replace, crop or enlarge finished material instead of accumulating small panels.
- **N47, N1 Section cards carry a big picture** (the object, person, figure or source page filling most of the frame) plus the section name only, no "SECTION" kicker; never text-only. The bridge sentence is spoken over it. Plus at most one evocative image beside the route strip; the explanatory diagram belongs on the next screen.
- **N65, N73 Title page and thumbnail.** Every video opens on a designed title page that works as a YouTube-style thumbnail: 3-6 large words promising what the viewer gets (a hook, not a heading), one striking real picture (zoomed capture, result, before and after), strong contrast, one accent colour, nothing else. Exported as `thumbnail.jpg` (1280x720); it is the first 2-3 s, its elements arriving. The source (title page) follows as evidence `[pre]`. The house card is `kit/samples/frames/title-card.json` (the ALT AGM opening): dark ground, a "Video summary" pill, the title on two lines with the second in the accent, a subtitle, a byline `Dominik Lukeš · <event> · <date>` and the picture in the panel on the right. After the render, `node tools/make-covers.mjs . --from mp4` cuts `cover.jpg`, `thumbnail.jpg` and `cover-og.jpg` from it. These covers are the companion's sharing image and the card on a talks site (author example: talks.dominiklukes.net), so no video ships without them. Dominik, 20 Nov 2026: the cover "should be a part of the skill and an expectation". A film without a title page fails `prerelease` `[pre]`. Section cards use the same visual language.

### Text on screen

- **D1 Text floor.** 34 px for any text; 26 px only for chart axis labels and source lines (`role: "axis"` or `"source"`); labels inside boxes 40 px or more. Replaces 4.4 (24 px) and N35 (26 px) `[lint]`.
- **N54, D4 No headings on screens.** No title, kicker, top-left key phrase or accent bar over a heading. Big text only on the title page, section cards and step cards. Titles and the route strip appear only on section cards. `titleMode` is `none`; the other modes (N8-N10, N13, N30) only when he asks for that video `[lint]`.
- **D5 Facts about who, when and where** (speaker, affiliation, event, date) are plain text, never a relationship drawing: "this does not need this kind of animation - text is enough" (13 Nov). Draw a relationship only when the narration states one (N87, N88) `[lint]`.
- **D6 Step cards.** A sequence (first step, next step) gets a step card, text 72 px or more, before its example screen. Silent videos use them throughout `[kit]`. Layout: the number about 200 px in the display face and the accent, a small "of N" on its baseline, the sentence below in the display face (96-104 px) on one shared left axis, an optional second clause in the accent, an optional picture on the right (640 px wide, rounded, centred on the text). No kicker, no title bar. Step, section and opening cards carry a picture or icon where the content has one (`picture`; 13 Nov: "the title pages should have some icons and illustrations") `[kit]`.
- **N22 Regular weight.** Quotes, statements, boxes, bars, lists, cycle nodes and steps are regular weight, sized up to fill the box. Bold only for short labels.
- **4.1, 4.2, N35 Short changing phrases.** At most 9 words per on-screen phrase; keep the outline's own text if longer and report it; shorten only by deleting words. Statement and quote screens show the sentence whole.
- **N59 No explanatory caption lines or framing labels** ("Agents named in the paper"). Labels name objects (product, axis, file); framing is spoken. No meta-labels, no event framing.
- **4.3 Code stays whole and still;** highlight the lines discussed. Terminal and trace turns arrive one at a time.
- **D10 Quote.** TalkWeaver's plain quote, the `quote` element: no box; words 64-80 px regular, width at most 1380 px, line height 1.24; pale oversized accent quotation mark (260 px, accent at 30 %) hanging left; source line in mono capitals, accent, 0.1em tracking, led by an em dash; optional portrait or screenshot left. No icon as a quote marker `[kit][lint]`.
- **D12 Stat.** `stat`: accent bold number about 150 px, label 48 px, no box; `stats` row of up to four `[kit]`.
- **9.1-9.5, N2 Look.** Tokens from `look.json` only (paper, ink, muted, hairline, accents, tints, fonts); no new colours, gradients or shadows. Fonts shipped and declared (`hyperframes-setup.md`); mono falls back to JetBrains Mono, say so. Contrast AA. Safe area x 120-1800, y 70-990, captions below. Icons: lucide inlined from `icons/` (pinned 1.21.0; add with `node tools/vendor-icons.mjs <name>`), one per meaning, never hand-drawn, never emoji.

### Boxes, connectors, highlights, chat

- **D2, N14, N23 Boxes.** A box with a label centres it both ways with at least 28 px between text and border; left-aligned rows only for chat messages and lists. Subtle: 2 px outline at about 34 % accent, 4-5 % tint; never a 4 px accent outline. A highlighted bar keeps 22-28 px padding inside its edge `[lint]`.
- **D3 Nothing overlaps text.** No badge, tab, chip, icon or highlight edge intersects another element's text box (an Agent tab never covers the label above its window) `[lint]`.
- **D7, N16, N5 Connectors.** Straight and meeting box edges square, or one smooth curve, starting and ending exactly on the borders with the head whole. A cycle's return is one curved path under the nodes, never a right-angle polyline. A label sits beside a connector with at least 16 px clear, never on it `[kit][lint]`.
- **D8 Pointers.** `pointer`: 4 px, ink or accent, never red, small head, curved when diagonal, ending at least 12 px from its target. No thick diagonal arrows; no dashed connectors except a tool call `[lint]`.
- **D9, 2.6, N90 One highlight style, `focus`.** Soft accent wash plus 4 px accent outline, radius 24, at least 24 px clear of the target's content on every side, in the orange accent (distinct from the green content colour); fades in over 0.35 s; the old target fades out as the new fades in. At most one change per 2 s ("flashes too much"); never re-highlight the current target; never highlight the only thing on screen. It follows the build, not the clock `[kit][lint]`. No strip, chip or label that echoes the narration; a cue with nothing in the picture to restate is dropped while the gap stays at 4 s or under.
- **N87 Group by meaning; never reset mid-relationship.** What belongs together in meaning stays in one view; a reset never separates a subject from its action, caption or result. Parts may arrive at separate cues. No fixed chunk sizes. Which items form a relationship, and which form draws it, is a judgement made per sentence and written into the design file, never inferred by code from counts or word lists. A plain list of peers stays a row of logo cards, smaller or in two rows, never wiped mid-sentence.
- **N88 Transformation first, then action arrow, then tag.** By meaning: (1) `transform` when something acts on, makes, turns, summarises, sorts or checks something: input as it looks, arrow into a small accent node with the verb, arrow out to the output; (2) `relate` for "X does Y" where nothing changes: subject card, verb in an accent pill on the arrow, result card; (3) `tagged` for "X is or has Y": a card per subject, what it is as a caption, what it has as tags, missing things crossed out in red. The picture keeps the narration's hedges ("virtually no", "in theory") in a caption or arrow label.
- **D11, N29 Chat.** Frames showing a chat use TalkWeaver's chat design, the `chat` element: grey user bubble right, agent text left with the small avatar, tool chips by kind. The kit's `agent` window (cast style, N36) keeps its tab inside the window's title bar. Trace draws the terminal-agent look. `message` padding at least 24 px, size at least 34 px.
- **D13 Frames are composed from kit elements** (`quote`, `stat`, `step`, `textblock`, `box`, `cycle`, `focus`, `pointer`, `chat`, relationship forms). A video's `frames-src/` sets content and positions; it may not hand-draw a quote, loop, highlight, chat, step card or pointer from raw rects, lines and text. A composition the kit lacks is added to the kit `[lint]`.

### Storyboard and gate

- **N44, N70 Storyboard before voice or render.** `node tools/storyboard.mjs .`: every state of every scene (one still per build), narration, arrival time, and a timeline strip per scene marking events against the narration so static stretches show; a draft MP4 alongside is better. The storyboard must be newer than the newest frame JSON `[pre]`. Dominik approves it before any voice or render.

## Step 8: plan and timing

- **2.1 When a build starts, in order of evidence:** logged `reveal` event (TalkWeaver run JSON); first word of the unit's phrase in the word timings; hand cue in `cues.json`; never invented (spread evenly, flag `fallback`, report).
- **2.2, 2.3 Never ahead of speech.** Builds start at the word onset (lead 0) and none before the transition into its scene ends (`ctx.t0`). List any logged reveal more than 1.5 s before its word and any clamped build.
- **2.4, 2.5 Order.** Units keep outline order; if spoken in another order, time each where it was said and report the inversion. A unit with no spoken match stays hidden until the end of the scene's speech (a heading shows from the start) and is reported.
- **N46 Speak over the transition.** "You need to speak over the transition. So there's a pause, nothing happens, and then a new screen opens." No silence at a scene change: the move starts on the last words of the outgoing scene or the first of the bridge, so the new screen settles while it is introduced. Scene starts come from word times (first word minus about 0.3 s), not clip ends.
- **N48, N69 Cadence.** Every sentence that adds a claim, name or number lands with something arriving at its word. `navigate`: a visual event (arrival, swap, move, highlight, new screen) every 2-3 s, never more than 4 s without one while the voice runs; a screen lasts about 6-10 s. `explain`: never more than 6 s. The build tool reports the largest gap per scene `[pre]`. Where something complicated is described, a highlight, pointer or zoom shows which part supports it.
- **7.3 A dwell over 25 s with no builds** is reported for his review, not decorated.

## Step 9: build

- **1.1, 1.5, 1.6 Every movement has a job:** direct attention to the item said, show a sequence or causal change, make a relation visible. No ambient motion (idle float, shimmer, particles, spin, drift). Process motion earns its place (a marker round a cycle, a line drawing between named steps, a bar growing to a named value).
- **1.3, 1.4 Frequency and speed apart.** Each move 0.45-0.6 s, ease-out, no bounce except `pop` on a short term. One build at a time; nothing starts within 0.5 s of the last unless both are named in the same breath.
- **6.1-6.4, N7, N8, N11 Transitions.** They mark a change of idea, between scenes only, never inside one or over a build. Default `push-left`, 1.2 s, sine.inOut; the other styles stay available per scene; an interlude crossfades in 0.5 s; a scene that `continues` cuts. No push when either scene is `calm`, the scene is under 4 s, it continues the idea, or the cut is mid-sentence on the same layout. No shaders; `whip-pan` and smearing shaders stay banned (`hyperframes-setup.md`). A title never crossfades into another title.
- **7 Long dwells in `calm`:** hold still; the voice and caption carry the frame. Allowed: the highlight moving to the unit spoken; on a single-image scene over 12 s, a push-in 1.00 to 1.04 or a crop toward the part pointed at (`crops`, from the speaker's words).
- **5.1-5.5 Captions.** For all narration, planned from the start, with `transcript.md` and `captions.vtt`. Clean recogniser output (`cleanCaptions` drops fillers and doubled words); fix slips with `captionFixes` after comparing with the outline's vocabulary, list each fix, leave unsure words and report them. Spell terms as the outline does. Time from word timings: one line, at most 8 words and 3.4 s, a new line at a pause over 0.7 s or a sentence end. Captions live outside the scenes, below y 990. Kit renders burn captions in.
- **10.1 Chapters.** `chapters.txt` from the section cards (N55, N56).
- **Kit layout defaults (do not hand-draw).** N3, N12: the block is centred vertically in the space it owns, and a card or window is sized to its content. For verbal-content slide templates: N15 compare rows regular weight with an icon each; N17 statement beside an image, the accent bar is the text block's own edge; N18 cards with an icon chip and a 38 px title over a thin accent rule; N19 trace at 36 px, never cut, scrolling by whole turns; N20 chat bubbles cascade top-left to bottom-right; N21 an image grid zooms into each image as discussed; N24 numbers centred on their text; N25 fill the boxes; N27 cards reveal point by point as highlight rows, no bullets; N28 banded tables.

## Step 10: pre-release check and stills

`node tools/prerelease.mjs .` must pass before a final render is published or sent (D14). A failure blocks; a warning needs a sentence in the build report. It prints the rules it did not check; look at those in the stills. Then open every still and look for: empty or text-only frames while the voice runs (N32); private details (N78); labels or numbers that disagree with their picture (N79); black areas from transparent PNGs (N82); logos drawn as solid boxes (N89); arrowheads and connector shape (D7); text touching a border or covered (D2, D3); heading-like text (D4); title page and thumbnail (N65, N73); small text (D1); fonts loading from `fonts/`.

## Step 11: render and review

- Render and loudness: `hyperframes-setup.md`, `voice.md`.
- **D15 Every comment he gives goes, the same day, into `feedback-ledger.md`** (optional local log, created on first use, not published) with its decision: kit default, check, rule, or this video only. A review note is a fix request at `scene.id` and `t`; reply to each.
- **D18 Typography.** House faces by token (`look.json` `type`, `fonts: {display, text, mono}`): the display face for big type (step cards, title page, section cards, textblock lines of 64 px or more, stat values), the text face for everything else; the quote words stay in the text face as in TalkWeaver, the quote mark in Georgia. Default `geist` (Dominik, 13 Nov: "very basic Arial and we should do better"; "I think we should use sans serif for videos"); presets `fraunces-alegreya` and `inter` remain for a video that asks for them. Check in a still that the face loaded, not a fallback `[kit]`.
- **D19 Measure.** Text runs 60-70 characters a line, centred, never the full width of a wide screen: textblock lines wrap at about 30 em (at most 1320 px); the chat window is at most 1320 px and centred; widget scenes are 1320 px wide and centred unless `fullWidth`. `lint-frames` warns on any text box over 1400 px (13 Nov: "the text should never go all the way across a wide screen") `[kit][lint]`.
- **D17 A fix made in a project's `kit/` copy goes back to the skill kit before the video is called done.** `new-video.mjs` fingerprints the kit (`kit/.kit-manifest.json`); `prerelease.mjs` lists every kit file changed since. Why: on 13 Nov the ALT AGM project held the 12 Nov zoom-jump fix, the picture clip and the turn motion that the skill never received, so the next video would have repeated the jumps. A fix to a screen composition the kit lacks becomes a kit element (D13), not project code.
