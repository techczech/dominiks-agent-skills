# Scoping a snack: domain map, then grilling

Two steps before any research run. The agent maps the field first, so that the questions it asks the author are informed. Then it interviews the author and records the answers. Keep both files in the snack's analysis folder, next to the research material and outside the published entry.

## Step 1: quick domain map (agent alone)

Time box: 5 to 10 minutes. This is a quick orientation, so that the questions are informed; do not go down routes the author has not steered yet. Mark gaps rather than filling them. The map can be deepened after the grilling, within the agreed exploration space. Write `domain-map.md`:

1. **The question** in the author's own words, with its source (post, paper, conversation).
2. **Terms.** The key concepts, with the competing definitions in use and who uses each one.
3. **Positions.** The main views on the question, who holds them and the key sources, every one linked.
4. **Methods.** How the field studies this: procedures, annotation schemes, measures. Give their known reliability and typical sample sizes.
5. **Data.** Corpora, datasets and annotated resources, with access and licence notes.
6. **Pitfalls.** Known traps: confounds, circular definitions, measures that do not mean what they seem.
7. **Gaps.** Where a quick check could add something, and what it could not show.
8. **Coverage.** What was searched, what was read in full, what was only skimmed and what was not checked.

Write it for a field expert who has not seen the conversation. Cite primary sources where possible. If a concept map helps, draw one. Mark anything uncertain as uncertain.

## Step 2: grilling (with the author)

Use the `grilling` skill: one question at a time, each with the agent's recommended answer drawn from the domain map. Cover these branches:

- **Current knowledge.** What the author already knows or believes, where it comes from, and how sure they are.
- **What they want to know.** The question they actually care about. What result would surprise them or change their mind?
- **Method preferences.** Methods they favour, methods they would not accept, and why.
- **Data ideas.** Sources they know of, their own data, and access they have.
- **Their part.** Anything only the author can do, such as coding a sample or judging borderline cases. Record the time they are willing to give.
- **Publication.** What may be quoted in their name.

Record the session in `brief.md` as it happens:

- **Decisions:** what was settled, in the author's words where they gave a view. Rule 14 depends on this record.
- **Open questions:** anything left for later or for the data to answer.
- **Exploration space:** directions the agent may pursue without asking, each with a time box. Log what each exploration found in `explorations.md`, including dead ends.

The grilling ends when the author confirms the brief. Research starts from the brief, not from the original request.

## Publishing the map

A deepened domain map is a research product in its own right. With the author's agreement, add a cleaned version to the snack's folder in the data repo and link it from the snack. Publish quotations from the grilling only where the author has approved the wording.
