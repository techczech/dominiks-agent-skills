# Language And Document Risks

Use this reference when preparing a target-language note, choosing a segmentation strategy, or deciding what a human reviewer should check.

## Language Research

Create one note per target language in the project workspace. Include:

- language, locale, and script
- intended readers
- literacy and readability constraints
- register expectations
- key terms and do-not-translate terms
- consent, legal, clinical, or stigma risks
- questionnaire wording risks
- community reviewer questions
- sources consulted, if web lookup is permitted

Do not use web lookup unless project consent allows it. If lookup is allowed, prefer authoritative and community-relevant sources over generic snippets.

## Document Types

Lay summaries: preserve plain-language meaning, audience fit, and consistent terminology. Segment by heading and paragraph. Do not invent headings.

Participant information sheets: preserve procedures, timing, eligibility, data use, withdrawal rights, risks, benefits, contacts, and institutional wording.

Consent forms: preserve legal and consent force. Segment by item. Do not soften or strengthen obligations.

Questionnaires: preserve item meaning, response scale anchors, time windows, negation, examples, and item numbering. Segment by item and scale.

Recruitment text: preserve invitation tone, eligibility, burden, compensation, deadlines, and contact details. Watch for coercive wording.

Debrief or results text: preserve explanation, reassurance, support contacts, and next steps.

Tables and forms: keep row and column relationships. Translate labels without breaking field names, response options, or form logic.

## Reviewer Questions

Ask specific questions:

- Does this phrase sound like an invitation rather than a requirement?
- Does this item preserve the time period in the source question?
- Is this wording plain enough for the intended reader group?
- Does this term carry stigma or clinical force that is not in the source?
