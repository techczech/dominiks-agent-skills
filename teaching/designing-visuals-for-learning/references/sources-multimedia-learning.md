# Sources and Evidence Limits

## Dominik's Framework and Authorial Intent

Dominik Lukeš, [Instructional Videos Framework](https://instructionalvideos.edutools.fyi/). Relevant sections: Six tasks of effective instructional videos; Principles of multimedia learning; Slide design; Videos compared with other modes; Readings.

The six tasks organise design decisions around attention, processing, integration, personal connection, control and contextual balance. The framework is a critical synthesis, not a production-technology manual. This skill retains those tasks and the contextual distinction between motion that guides attention and motion that competes with processing.

The framework's readings include Clark and Mayer (2016), *e-Learning and the Science of Instruction*, fourth edition; Koumi (2006), *Designing Video and Multimedia for Open and Flexible Learning*; Mayer (2005), *The Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*; and Mayer (2011), *Multimedia Learning*, [Oxford Bibliographies](https://doi.org/10.1093/obo/9780199756810-0026). These are the framework's cited lineage; listing them does not mean every underlying study was rechecked for this update.

## Research Basis

Richard E. Mayer (2021), *Multimedia Learning*, third edition, Cambridge University Press. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316941355); [publisher frontmatter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97813166/38088/frontmatter/9781316638088_frontmatter.pdf).

The publisher's frontmatter identifies the edition, contents and attention to boundary conditions. It supports treating the principles as research-based and conditional; it is not a substitute for reading a chapter or study when a specific effect is at issue. The operational checks in this skill adapt Dominik's synthesis. Do not promise effect sizes or universal benefits from them.

Barbara Tversky, Julie Bauer Morrison and Mireille Bétrancourt (2002), *Animation: can it facilitate?*, International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 57, 247–262. [Author-hosted paper](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/bt2158/faculty-profile/files/_Morrison_Betrancourt_AnimationCanitfacilitate.pdf).

This review examines whether animated and static comparisons are equivalent and whether learners can perceive the displayed events. It supports caution about fast, complex transient displays. It does not establish that every animation is harmful or that any given modern interface benefits from motion. Dominik's attention-guiding-motion advice remains a contextual design judgement.

## Accessibility Basis

W3C Web Accessibility Initiative, [Planning Audio and Video Media](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/planning/), and its linked guidance on captions, transcripts and visual description. Use it to plan access alongside the explanation. Captions are not interchangeable with a transcript; visual information may also need description.

For a formal accessibility-conformance claim, inspect the applicable standards and the actual output. This skill's practical checks do not certify conformance.

## What the Extension Adds

The cross-medium adaptations, relationship-label guidance, source/interpretation boundaries, overview-before-detail and internal-summary-before-source-navigation patterns are operational design guidance. They are consistent with the six tasks; they are not presented as separate Mayer principles or experimentally proven universal sequences.

Worked examples are invented teaching scenarios with stated assumptions. Validation cases assess whether agents apply the guidance sensibly. Neither is evidence of measured learner improvement.
