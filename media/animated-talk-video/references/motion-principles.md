# Motion principles

Read before choosing a template or writing any GSAP. Terse on purpose. Each rule names its source.

Sources. Bracketed tags after a rule say where it comes from; the rule text stands on its own, so you do not need the sources:
- **S** = Dominik's skill `designing-instructional-videos` (SKILL.md). **P** = its `references/principles.md` (Mayer-style multimedia-learning checks, restated). Both are private; the rules below carry what you need.
- **A** = Dominik's private design note on motion sketches (edutools.fyi ADR 0003). **F** = his Instructional Videos Framework (2023).
- **N** = `hyperframes-setup.md` (facts about the renderer).
- **N1-N30** (numbered notes at the end) = the house style: Dominik's own review decisions on drafts of this kit, quoted. Where a note and an earlier rule disagree, the note wins.

Framing: a video is an illustrated explanation, never the slides redrawn. The slides are the key points; the video shows what they are about (people, actions, objects, the real interface, the talk's own charts) while the voice explains. Two genres: **instruction** (a recorded talk recap, a tutorial: learning outcome = what the talk said) and **illustrated orientation** (getting people to understand and want something, such as the agents video for professional services, N31). Both follow the same evidence rules: the talk, the outline and the speaker's own examples are the evidence (S "Build from Evidence"). Sections 11-14 (N31-N70) are the current house style and win over older rules where they disagree. For an outline-only video (no recording) the outline and its narration script are the evidence, and every rule that says "speaker" applies to the narration.

## 1. When motion is allowed, and how much

- **1.1 Every movement has a job.** Allowed jobs: direct attention to the item being said; show a sequence or causal change; make a relation visible. No job, no movement. [P "What Animation Is For", list "Animation has especially strong explanatory value when it"; P "Six Design Tasks" 1 "as signals, not decoration"]
- **1.2 Two levels only: `calm` and `standard`.** `calm` = fade only, no travel, no highlight, no count-up, no marker. `standard` = fade plus <= 28 px travel, a "now" highlight on the item being spoken about, markers on cycles. Pick per slide with `suggestMotion(slide)`, then override from the audience. [P "What Animation Is For" paras 2 and 3; S "Design the Explanation" 4; A Decision 4]
- **1.3 Set frequency and speed separately.** Focus may change as often as the speaker's ideas do. Each individual move stays slow: 0.45-0.6 s, ease-out, no bounce except `pop` on a short term (<= 0.6 s). [P "What Animation Is For" last paragraph: "frequency and speed separately"]
- **1.4 Nothing else moves while a build settles or the learner reads.** One build at a time; no second element starts within 0.5 s of the last one unless the speaker named both in the same breath. [P "Six Design Tasks" 2 "pause when the learner needs processing time"]
- **1.5 No ambient or looping motion.** No idle float, shimmer, particles, continuous spin, background drift on text or diagram slides. [P "Six Design Tasks" 2 "Remove decorative images, motion"; P "What Animation Is For" "constant decorative movement can overload a novice"]
- **1.6 Motion that shows a process is the exception that earns its keep.** A marker running round a cycle as the speaker names the stages, a line drawing between two named steps, a bar growing to a named value. [P "What Animation Is For" "shows movement or manipulation... reveals a sequence"]

## 2. Build timing

- **2.1 Order of evidence for when a build starts:** (1) the logged `reveal` event for that slide (TalkWeaver run JSON only); (2) the first word of the unit's own phrase in the word timings (speech match); (3) a hand cue in `cues.json` (`--cues`); (4) never invented. If only (4) is left, spread evenly and put it in the report. [`scripts/plan-from-timings.mjs` `source` field: reveal-event, speech-match, hand-cue, fallback]
- **2.2 Never ahead of speech.** Speech-matched and hand-cued builds start at the word onset (lead 0), not before. A logged reveal is used as logged, but list any reveal that comes more than 1.5 s before its word. [P "Multimedia-Learning Checks" "Temporal contiguity: explain an event while it is visible"; S "Design the Explanation" 3 "where attention must be directed"]
- **2.3 No build starts before the transition into its slide has finished.** `ctx.t0` already enforces this. Report any build clamped by it. [S "Review Gate" "motion... without competing with processing"]
- **2.4 Do not reorder content to fit the speech.** Units keep outline order. If the speaker said them in a different order, time each unit where the speaker said it and report the inversion. [S "Build from Evidence" "never patch an evidence gap"]
- **2.5 A unit with no spoken match stays hidden until the end of the slide's speech is near, or is shown from the start if it is a heading.** Never reveal a unit the speaker did not talk about as if the speaker had. Report it.
- **2.6 The "now" highlight follows the build, not the clock.** On at the build time, off at the next build, at most 6 s. Slides that stay up long after the last build (lists, columns) may instead carry `highlights` cues (`{item, at}` from the words where the speaker names or returns to an item; `item: null` clears): the item is tinted and the other built items dim to 62 %, 0.45 s, no scale; no cue where nothing is named (`kit.focus`, list, iconrow and compare). [P "Six Design Tasks" 1 "Point to the exact item being discussed"; P "Multimedia-Learning Checks" "Signalling"]
- **2.7 Headings and the rule draw at the cut, once.** Words in a section title may stagger (<= 0.18 s each). [N "Structure an agent would generate"]

## 3. What may be added beyond the slide

- **3.1 Add only what the speaker says aloud and the slide cannot show by itself.** Example: the brain / hands / computer / eyes analogy on "The model and the harness", drawn as the speaker names each, in its own interlude scene (rule N4). Use the `analogy` template, which refuses a node without `t` and `said`. [S "Build from Evidence" "Every claim about what can be done needs visible evidence"; P "Multimedia-Learning Checks" "Coherence"]
- **3.2 An addition is a scene of its own, not a layer on a dense slide.** It uses the speaker's words for labels and never covers slide text (rule N4). [P "Six Design Tasks" 2 "Show only the graphic or screen area currently being explained"; "Spatial contiguity"]
- **3.3 Log every addition** in the report: what, where, the quoted words, the time. [S "Build from Evidence" "preserve provenance" (and A Decision 7)]
- **3.4 Never add:** facts, numbers or names the speaker did not say; stock footage; music or sound effects; lower-thirds; slogans; calls to action; decorative shapes; a "better" diagram of what the speaker said. Allowed since N36-N39: line-art illustration of what the speaker describes, the talk's own widgets and charts, and official logos for products and companies the speaker names. [S "First Move" "Stop and reframe if the concept begins with a slogan, logo reveal"; S "Design the Explanation" 6 "Prefer the actual interface... over decorative metaphor"; S "Design the Explanation" 7 "Avoid promotional calls to action"]
- **3.5 If the outline and his speech disagree, show the outline and report the difference.** Do not correct either. [S "Build from Evidence" "Inspect the real artefact"]

## 4. On-screen text and narration

- **4.1 Short changing phrases, never the spoken sentence.** A unit is <= 9 words. `lint()` warns above that. If outline text is longer, keep it (it is the speaker's slide) and report it; shorten only by deleting words from the outline's own text, never by paraphrase. [S "Design the Explanation" 5 "Do not repeat the full spoken sentence as display text"; P "Multimedia-Learning Checks" "Redundancy"; P "What Animation Is For" "Prefer short changing phrases over full duplicate captions"]
- **4.2 Statement and quote slides are the exception.** The sentence is the slide. Show it whole; reveal a second line only when the speaker reaches it. [S "Design the Explanation" 6]
- **4.3 Code stays whole and still.** Highlight the lines the speaker discusses; do not type it out. Terminal and trace turns appear one at a time because the sequence is the content. [P "What Animation Is For" "Let the learner inspect a stable diagram, equation, interface state or code example"]
- **4.4 Keep text >= 24 px at 1920x1080, body mostly >= 30 px.** Shrink via `fit()` before cropping; if it still does not fit, shorten the slide, not the font. [P "Six Design Tasks" 2 "Simplify, crop, enlarge"]

## 5. Captions

- **5.1 Captions run for all narration.** Plan them and a transcript from the start. [S "Preserve Learner Control"; P "Video Versus Other Modes" "A transcript is a learning resource"]
- **5.2 Clean the recogniser output; never show fillers.** `cleanCaptions()` drops uh, um, er, ah, hm and doubled words. Fix recogniser slips with `fixes` after comparing the line with the outline's vocabulary and the slide on screen (a recogniser once heard "the computer" as "hard computer"). When unsure leave the words and report them. Spell terms as the outline does. [P "Six Design Tasks" 6 "access, clarity"; Dominik's brief for this kit]
- **5.3 Time captions from word timings, not interpolated segments.** One line, <= 8 words, <= 3.4 s, a new line at a pause > 0.7 s or a sentence end.
- **5.4 Captions live outside the scenes and below y = 990.** Shader transitions capture the scene DOM; captions inside would smear. [N "Fought" "Captions must live outside the scene elements"]
- **5.5 Write `captions.vtt` and a clean `transcript.md` next to the MP4.** [S "Preserve Learner Control"]

## 6. Transitions

- **6.1 A transition marks a change of idea.** Between slides only, starting at the logged change and ending at change + its duration (builds never start before it ends; the review page lands there). Never inside a slide, never over a build. [P "What Animation Is For" "a restrained transition between examples"]
- **6.2 Default: `push-left`** (1.2 s, sine.inOut), done with GSAP on the scene elements (`templates/transitions.mjs`); no shader is used. The other five styles stay in the repertoire and are chosen per scene (`scene.transition`). An inserted interlude crossfades in (0.5 s); a slide that `continues` cuts. [N11]
- **6.3 Shaders are gone.** `compose()` no longer loads HyperShader; a scene that still asks for one gets `push-left` and a warning. `whip-pan` and the other smearing shaders stay banned: on near-white slides they smear the text and produce a blank frame (`hyperframes-setup.md`).
- **6.4 No push, crossfade or cut, when:** either slide is `calm`; the scene is shorter than 4 s (flick-through); the slide `continues` the previous idea; the cut is mid-sentence and the slides share a layout. [P "What Animation Is For" para 2; S "Review Gate" "Is the amount of motion appropriate"]

## 7. Long dwells

Decision: **hold still.** The voice and the changing caption carry the frame. Do not add decorative motion to keep a slide "alive". Why: the sanctioned way to refresh a frame is a "slow crop or highlight" that is subordinate to the explanation (P "What Animation Is For" para 3), and motion without a job overloads (P "What Animation Is For" last paragraph). Allowed during a dwell:
- **7.1** the "now" highlight moving to the unit the speaker is on (standard only);
- **7.2** on a single-image slide, a push-in 1.00 to 1.04 over the dwell (standard only, > 12 s), or a crop toward a part the speaker points at (`crops`, only with the speaker's words as source);
- **7.3** nothing else. A dwell > 25 s with no builds is reported, not decorated: it is a review note for Dominik.
- **7.4** Re-highlighting a unit when the speaker returns to it: use highlight cues (2.6), especially on long dwells.

## 8. Dense or beginner content gets less motion

- **8.1 Use `calm`** when: the slide has > 60 words or > 7 items; the layout is `code` or `table`; the slide defines a term for the first time; the audience is beginners in this topic. Calm also turns shaders into plain crossfades. [P "What Animation Is For" para 2 "For beginners learning important, unfamiliar and conceptually dense material... stillness is often better"; S "Design the Explanation" 4]
- **8.2 Use `standard`** for orientation slides, section openers, short lists, recaps. [P "What Animation Is For" para 3]
- **8.3 One new concept per scene.** Do not merge slides to make a busier scene. [P "Six Design Tasks" 3 "Introduce one new concept per coherent segment"]
- **8.4 Order of explanation = order of build.** Purpose before detail: the title and rule arrive first, then units. [P "Six Design Tasks" 3 "Explain the purpose before the detail"]
- **8.5 If the audience is not stated in the package, assume general orientation (`standard`) and say so in the report.** A talk that teaches a hard new concept needs `calm`; ask Dominik, do not guess.

## 9. Colour and type: the talk's own look

- **9.1 Use only tokens from the look file (`look.json`):** paper, ink, muted, hairline, accent colours, tints, font families. Accent per section as the deck gives it. No new colours, no gradients, no shadows. [S "Build from Evidence" "Inspect the real artefact"; P "Multimedia-Learning Checks" "Coherence"]
- **9.2 Ship the fonts and declare `@font-face` (see `hyperframes-setup.md`, Fonts).** The renderer ignores system fonts (N "Fought" "Fonts"). Mono falls back to JetBrains Mono unless a mono file is bundled; say which you used.
- **9.3 Keep contrast at AA** (`hyperframes check` reports it). [S "Preserve Learner Control" (access)]
- **9.4 Safe area:** x 120-1800, y 70-990. Captions sit below.
- **9.5 Icons are lucide, inlined SVG** from `icons/` (pinned lucide-static 1.21.0, the version the kit's icons came from). Never hand-drawn glyphs, never emoji (rule N2).

## 10. Chapters and learner control

- **10.1 Emit `chapters.txt` from the `##` section slides** (time + title). [S "Preserve Learner Control" "meaningful chapter titles and seekable boundaries"; P "Six Design Tasks" 5]

## Dominik's decisions: slide vs animated scene (2026-10-16)

His words in quotes, then the rule. (p-numbers refer to slides in his review of the draft.)

- **N11 Default transition.** "1 Push left". Rule: `push-left` (1.2 s, sine.inOut) is the default between scenes; the other five stay available. Scene clips are visible from their `start`; the new scene's builds begin after the move ends; `scenes.json` carries `settle` (= start + move length) and the review page jumps there, never into the middle of a move. (`index.mjs` `pickTransition`, `compose().moves`, `kit/tools/review-page.mjs`)
- **N12 Vertical centring.** "vertically centred" (p3, p4, p5), "the graphics should be more centred vertically" (N3, still true). Rule: the content block of every layout is centred in the space it owns: under the title block, or on the whole slide when there is no title (quote, statement without body lines, iconlist/numbered/list with the title column on the left). With a kicker, tab or no title the block moves up (`TITLE_DY`). A card, panel or window is sized to its content, not to the band, so there is no dead lower half (`cards`, `compare`, `code` trace).
- **N13 No kicker over the title.** "too distracting", "no small kicker over the title unless necessary" (p4, p5, p12, p13). Rule: the small line above a title is drawn only when the slide supplies `slide.kicker`; `slide.section` is no longer shown. Without it the title block sits 34 px lower (`.topbar.nk`) so the centring still holds. The `list` layout's `.ltitle` follows the same rule and also honours the title modes: `full` (title column left), `kicker`, `tab`, `none`, `intro-then-none` (the last three put the list under a small block or nothing, wide and centred).
- **N14 Subtle boxes.** "more subtle boxes around text" (p6). Rule: boxes around text have a 2 px outline in about 34% accent mixed into white (ink at 26% for neutral nodes) and a 4-5% accent tint; never a 4 px accent outline. Applies to `iconrow`, `cycle`, `process`, `cards`, `compare`, `chat`, `analogy`, image-claim boxes.
- **N15 Compare and contrast.** "not bold text and add icons - also no subtitle over title" (p12, p13). Rule: rows are regular weight (headings stay bold mono); every row carries a lucide icon (compare: `children[].icon`; contrast: `item.icons = [left, right]`); a missing icon is reported as a warning. No kicker (N13).
- **N16 Connectors touch the boxes.** "the lines need to actually touch the boxes" (p21); cycle: "but arrows with curves" (p9). Rule: a connector starts and ends exactly on the borders of the boxes it joins (`edge()` gap 0; the arrowhead marker's `refX` is its tip, `arrowMarker()` in `_kit.mjs`), so there is no floating end and the head is whole. This replaces the 12-16 px gap of N5; the head is still never hidden. Cycle connectors are arcs of the ring itself, from where the ring leaves one box to where it enters the next (bisection on the box rectangles). iconrow's chain links run from border to border. Image-claim: each claim box has a line from its left edge to the picture's right edge, head on the picture.
- **N17 Statement with an image.** "add the image but title centre like this but the line next to title needs to be aligned with it" (p2). Rule: `slide.images[0]` sits beside the centred text block; the accent bar is the text block's own left edge, so it is exactly as tall as the text and moves with it.
- **N18 Cards title.** "but title needs to be better formatted" (p14). Rule: each card's title row is an icon chip (tinted rounded square) and a 38 px ink title over a thin accent rule, then the points; the card height follows its content.
- **N19 Trace shows everything.** "text size from scene but animate so all data shows up" (p18). Rule: trace uses the scene's larger text (36 px) and is never cut or shrunk to fit. The panel is a window; each turn arrives at its build time and, when the pile no longer fits, the pile scrolls up by whole turns (0.8 s, power2.inOut) so the newest turn is complete and nothing is clipped mid-turn.
- **N20 Chat cascade.** "but descending from left to right" (p19). Rule: bubbles step down from the top-left to the bottom-right, whoever speaks (the label and colour say who); the step is `min(300, (1680 - 1020) / (n - 1))` px.
- **N21 Image grid zooms.** "zoom in on the images as you animate don't show the slide as is" (p22). Rule: the grid builds image by image; as each is discussed the camera moves into it (1.4 s sine.inOut, then a 5% drift until the next one), earlier images fade back, and the last move returns to the whole grid. Needs one spoken item per image; without that the grid stays whole and a warning is raised.
- **Kept as the slide's own:** code (p17); the grid slide itself (p22), whose animated scene is the zoom in N21.

## 11. Round 5: illustrated videos (professional services agents video, 2026-10-28 to 11-03; recorded-talk pilot 2026-11-04)

Source: the "Illustrating AI Agents for Professional Services" thread and its approved storyboard (`dominiks-presentations-library/agents-2026/preparing-your-institution-for-agents/design/storyboard-v3/visual-system.md`, frames in `frames/`, generator in `src/`). His words in quotes, then the rule. These win over sections 1-10 and N1-N30 where they disagree.

- **N31 Genre first.** "this is slightly different from explaining something to people who need to learn. It's more kind of illustrating, getting people excited about something." Rule: before planning, name the genre (instruction or illustrated orientation) and the audience ("people who need the strategic background and not the technical detail"). Orientation is not promotion: no slogans or calls to action, but it may be lively and it carries the speaker's point of view.
- **N32 Never an empty or waiting screen while someone speaks.** "when there's a transition and you land on a blank page, there actually shouldn't be. There should be something arriving on that page." Rule: every scene lands with its main visual already arriving; nothing is on screen for more than about 1 s with only a title or one short line while the voice runs. A sparse bullet scene (two words on white) is a failure, not calm design: illustrate it (N33) or merge it into the scene it belongs to. Check every settled still for this before render.
- **N33 Everything is illustrated.** "it needs to be more visual - everything needs to be illustrated - e.g. someone thinking about a problem, someone typing search into a search box, etc. then risk register needs to be shown etc. the slides are the key points but they need something else to support them in video". Rule: each spoken idea gets a picture of the thing itself: a person doing the action, the object (document, drawer, register, inbox), the real interface state, or the talk's chart. Slide text appears only as the one key phrase (N35). Text-only templates (`list`, `statement`, `cards`) are for genuinely verbal content (a quotation, a definition), not the default.
- **N34 What is spoken about is on the screen, and the picture does the saying.** "when you're illustrating something, what you're speaking about is on the screen. So, for example, the first screen when it says agents, agents, agents, that should fill up the whole screen. And you shouldn't have to say agents". Rule: voice and image have different jobs (`designing-instructional-videos` "Plan the spoken story"). The picture shows the thing at a scale that fills the frame; the voice says what it means. One main focus per frame; replace or enlarge rather than adding small panels.
- **N35 One key phrase per frame.** From the approved visual system: one phrase, at most nine words, top left; labels >= 26 px; caption zone below y 990 stays empty; floor line at y 940.
- **N36 Line-art illustration system.** Approved 2026-11-03: ink outlines on paper, one accent (kit green), red only for errors. A recurring main character with the same face in every frame (only pose and mood change), a few secondary characters, varied extras. The agent is always a chat window with an "Agent" tab and visible tool calls (dashed arrows with a chip naming the tool); no robots, no faces on AI. What the model sees is drawn as a tray ("Model's context") that only fills with what tools return. The kit ships the generator (JS port of that project's `cast.py` / `props.py`): frame JSON, `illustration` template, `references/inputs.md` "Frame JSON"; keep characters consistent within a video.
- **N37 Demonstrate processes as ordinary actions.** "use a normal user search as a scenario not cli and then the agent use a normal chatbot with tool reveals; the clis are too obscure"; "my example of the agent searching vs human searching ... should be core to understand how simple agents are - they're not magic". Rule: show a process as the steps a person would recognise (type a query, open a drawer, read a file, choose the next step), then the same steps done by the agent as visible tool calls. No terminal unless the audience is technical.
- **N38 Products with logos; progress shown as a chart.** "add some products with logos ... to show the progression and also illustrate how quickly things are moving this year"; "illustrate on gpt model Artificial Analysis score increase". Rule: a named product or company carries its official mark, taken from TalkWeaver's bundled sets (`compiler/assets/icons/`: `svgl.json`, `simple-icons.json`, `extra.json`), never redrawn (same rule as `talkweaver-visuals`). Progress over time is a real chart from the `ai-model-benchmarks` data, not a list. Supersedes N26's "use a lucide icon" fallback whenever the TalkWeaver set has the mark.
- **N39 Reuse the talk's widgets and charts.** When the talk already has an HTML widget or chart for the idea (TalkWeaver `talkweaver-visuals` widgets, `ai-model-benchmarks` charts), the scene shows that widget at 1920x1080, built up in step with the speech, rather than a redrawn bullet version. Widgets made for the room already follow the right rules (no caveats on the face, logos, large labels). A slide that is only a live embed is not skipped: capture the widget or the page state and use it.
- **N40 Narrative before script, script before pictures.** "Outline the whole narrative story first, then have another look at it. So don't do it at once ... Think about what does the audience need to know? How am I bringing them along?" Rule: save the audience's starting knowledge, the claims to establish and a narrative outline as files; review them; then write one connected section's spoken draft together with its visual states; review; then the next. Voice and render only after the whole script and storyboard are reviewed. For a recorded talk, the excerpts are the script: choose them for the through-line, then storyboard pictures for each excerpt.
- **N41 Narrate the development, not the numbering.** "when you say climbing the capability ladder, you don't say step one ... you say, okay, first we did this. Then, as AI got better, we moved on to that". Rule: no "step one, step two", no category labels read aloud; tell what changed and why.
- **N42 No hedges, no "what it does not mean", no AI patterns in narration.** "do not say what it doesn't mean"; "Do not ever say things like this measures model capability ... do not put hedges in, just say this is the index ... they can do more ... they still make errors, but that's fine". Rule: write narration with `expository-writing` for the genre of spoken video, then an `unslop` pass; plain claims, errors described in ordinary words ("it can make errors, but they are similar to human errors"). Do not explain dips or exceptions the audience does not need.
- **N43 Diagrams follow the real-world metaphor and the content authority.** "the ladder is upside down - we're climbing up to 4"; ladder rungs Ask, Retrieve, Delegate, Always on; rungs 1-2 labelled Chatbot, Agent only from rung 3. Rule: a metaphor is drawn as the real thing behaves (a ladder is climbed upwards); level names and labels come from the speaker, verbatim.
- **N44 Storyboard gate.** Before voicing or rendering, show Dominik a storyboard page (every frame as a still, its key phrase, its narration and its movement, as in `storyboard-agents-video-v3.html`). Render only after he approves it. Synthetic voice, when he asks for one, runs slightly faster than default ("speed up the voice a little bit").

## 12. Round 6: paper explainer review (Feng et al. 2026 video, 2026-11-06)

Source: his review of the first render of the Feng et al. explainer (published at https://agent-primer-nature.semanticmachines.fyi/). His words in quotes, then the rule. These win over sections 1-11 where they disagree.

- **N45 Two modes: explain and navigate.** "two modes. One of them is an explainer ... to beginners of very complicated things where we need to be much more judicious about what's on the screen. One of them is kind of a more short ... navigation that tries to underscore more points." Rule: choose the mode with the genre (N31), before the narrative, and record it in the outline frontmatter (`mode: explain` or `mode: navigate`).
  - `explain`: beginners meeting a hard idea. One concept per screen, few elements, `calm`, longer holds, worked examples. Still never an empty screen (N32).
  - `navigate`: a guided tour of a paper, report or talk for people who want its points. More beats, a visual arriving for every point (N48), a new screen whenever the elaboration changes (N49), `standard` motion, concrete cases from the source (N51). Paper and report explainers default to `navigate`.
- **N46 Speak over the transition.** "You need to speak over the transition. So there's a pause, nothing happens, and then a new screen opens." Rule: no silence at a scene change. The voice runs on; the move into the next screen starts on the last words of the outgoing scene or the first words of the bridge sentence, so the new screen settles while it is being introduced. Generate voice per section (several scenes in one take) so the bridge keeps its intonation; trim clip silences so no gap is longer than about 0.35 s; set scene starts from the word times (the first word of the scene's narration minus about 0.3 s), not from clip ends. This replaces the speech-gap rule in `agent-brief.md` section 6.
- **N47 Section cards carry a big picture.** "we need the interstitial slides to have big visuals so that it's clear as we're moving from one part of the argument to the other." Rule: every section opens on an interstitial card: one large picture that fills most of the frame (the object, the person, the figure or a page of the source) plus the section name. The bridge sentence (N50) is spoken over it. Never a text-only section card.
- **N48 Punctuate every point with a visual.** "we need to punctuate new points being added by a visual being added. So we should have no long periods of the video where nothing is seen." Rule: each new point (in practice each sentence that adds a claim, name or number) lands with something arriving at its spoken word: an element, a highlight, a zoom or a new screen. Gaps without a visual change: at most about 6 s in `navigate`, about 10 s in `explain`. While something complicated is described, a highlight, pointer or zoom shows which part supports it.
- **N49 A new screen for each elaboration; no busy screens.** "those slides get too busy"; "when we have two different elements that elaborate on screen, we should definitely switch to a new screen, sort of redraw the screen and start putting things in." Rule: one screen carries one elaboration. When the narration turns to a second element (another failure, another example, another stage), move to a fresh screen and build again rather than adding a panel. Rough cap: five built elements per screen, one main focus.
- **N50 Narrated links.** "we need to make narrated links between the points. So, in an introduction and then narrate when we sort of move on to the next point. So, the story needs to kind of flow." Rule: open with an introduction that says where the video is going; start each section with a bridge sentence that links the last point to the next ("That is the frame. What does such a system actually do?"); split a difficult concept over several screens, each with a concrete example. Bridges are spoken, never written as on-screen text.
- **N51 The picture may elaborate, not echo.** "sometimes what is on the screen doesn't have to be exactly what's being said, as in the same words, but it could be a visual that explains it, that sort of somehow elaborates it." Rule: a screen may show an example, case, figure or table row that explains the sentence rather than restating it, provided it comes from the source (rule 3.4 still holds). For a paper, prefer the concrete systems and results it describes ("what people are talking about") over the abstract framework alone: name the system, show what it did, cite the page.
- **N52 Show where it comes from.** "we should also have screenshots from the paper, like the paper's title page and things like that. So, it says where it's from." Rule: a paper or report explainer opens on the source itself (an image of its title page, with journal, authors and year legible) and shows the page, figure or table it draws on when it does, with a small page chip. Check the source's licence: a paywalled or copyrighted page is fine for internal teaching use; mark the video as not cleared for public hosting.
- **N53 Mine the companion explainers.** "some of the explainers in the HTML can also be part of this ... that actually are missing a little bit from the video." Rule: when a guide, visual guide or widget set already exists for the same source, take scenes from its panels and widgets (N39) before inventing new pictures, and list which ones you used.

## 13. Round 7: storyboard v2 review (Feng et al. video, 2026-11-06, 11:00)

His annotated screenshots of storyboard v2, quoted, then the rule. These win over sections 1-12 where they disagree. The general rule behind them (performance plus self-study companion) lives in `designing-visuals-for-learning`, section "Pair Every Performance with a Self-Study Companion"; this section is how the video side applies it.

- **N54 No PowerPoint titles on explainer screens.** "no PowerPoint style titles"; "the title on the slide should not be there while the slide is on"; "this makes it look like a PowerPoint slide". Rule: explainer screens carry no title, kicker or key phrase. The picture and the voice do the work. This replaces the `kicker` default of N30 and the key phrase of N35 for screens: `titleMode` defaults to `none`, and frame JSON `key` is empty on screens.
- **N55 Section cards mark progress with a lead-in title.** "put these in interstitial slides to mark progress not on explainer slides to compete with explanations"; "the lead in title should have some words that lead the person in not cryptic cute language like 'the route' ... Instead of the problem it should be what we are trying to solve"; "the single word titles make it a PowerPoint and also sound like AI language". Rule: the progress marker (the route strip with the current part lit) and the title appear only on section cards, never on screens. A card title is a 4-9 word lead-in built on a verb or a question word ("What problem the paper sets out to solve", "How these systems work, step by step"). Never a single noun. The card's picture is the route itself, drawn large, plus at most one evocative image. The explanatory diagram belongs on the next screen, not on the card.
- **N56 Titles are chapters.** "we need to have strong clickable titles for easy discovery". Rule: the card titles are the video's chapter titles (`chapters.txt`) and the companion page's clickable table of contents. Write them for someone scanning a list: each says what the part answers.
- **N57 Conversational bridges, verbs not nouns.** "this slide should not need to restate the title - you should be much more conversational about it as a real presenter which means verbs not nouns". Rule: the narration over a card bridges from the last part in a presenter's voice. It never reads out or paraphrases the card title ("So, the problem."), and it uses verbs ("To close that gap, the paper proposes ..."), not noun labels.
- **N58 One slide, one point.** "one slide one point". Rule: a screen shows one point. Two pictures that make two points (a pipeline and a locked lab track) are two screens, or one replaces the other with `hide`. This tightens N49.
- **N59 Sidebar text belongs in the narration.** "keep sidebars to narration" (on a caption line "Agents named in the paper" above a logo row). Rule: no explanatory caption lines, headers or framing labels on screens ("Agents named in the paper", "Report", "Reasoning"). Labels name objects (a logo's product name, an axis, a file name); framing is spoken.
- **N60 Widgets in video mode.** "when showing widgets don't show the full ui that end users use but not video audience"; on the widget's step caption: "for self-study not for video". Rule: a widget scene loads the widget with `?video=1`: figure only, scaled to fill the frame, with no header, step buttons, Notes, About, Reset or caption. The full interface stays in the widget's default (self-study) mode for the companion page. If a widget has no video mode, add one (`talkweaver-visuals`); do not crop around the chrome.
- **N61 Screenshots large, with zoom.** "screenshots should take more space"; "zoom in on screenshots". Rule: a page or table screenshot fills most of the frame, and the camera zooms into the region being spoken about (a scale-and-pan on the image, 1.2-1.6 s ease), rather than a highlight box on a small page.
- **N62 Logos for every named product.** "find logo" (Hermes Agent shown as a name only). Rule: look for the official mark beyond TalkWeaver's sets: the product's own site or repository, saved under the project's `assets/logos/` with its source URL in `PROVENANCE`/README. Fall back to a name tile only when no official mark exists, and say so.
- **N63 Every video ships with its companion page.** "every time there also to comply with the multimedia learning principles to give learner control ... when we embed the video into the page, it should always each section should then distribute a table of contents, and then people should always be able to ... explore themselves". Rule: the deliverable is the MP4 plus a companion HTML page: the video embedded, the card titles as a clickable table of contents that seeks the video, and under each section a step-through of its screens (the same frames as stepped figures, with titles and notes), the widgets in self-study mode, and sources. Build it from the same outline and frames, so the two cannot drift apart.
- **N64 A narrative by a person explaining.** "the script needs work to be more cohesive and explicit. 'Start with how research works.' We need some proper sentences to link things. Think about cohesion, not just slogans. This is a narrative by a person explaining, not a PR promo." Rule: narration is continuous explanatory prose in full sentences, with explicit links between ideas (why the next point matters, what it answers, how it follows or contrasts). Read across scene boundaries it must sound like one person talking. No telegraphic fragments, one-line slogans or promo cadence. Short pictures-first screens do not license short, clipped narration. Write it with `expository-writing`, then a cohesion pass (`cohesion-review`: reference chains, given-before-new ordering, connectives), then `unslop`. Read the whole script aloud in order before voicing. Tightening for length must keep the links: cut points, never connectives.

## 14. Round 8: first v2 render (Feng et al. video, 2026-11-06, 13:30)

His review of the rendered v2, quoted, then the rule. These win over sections 1-13 where they disagree. In particular they replace the "hold still" default of section 7 for `navigate` videos.

- **N65 Open on a title card, not a screenshot.** "it shouldn't start with the paper screenshot, it should start with a proper title that has the key things, what it's about." Rule: the first scene is a title card that names the subject in large type, with a picture of what it is about. The source (title page) comes next, as evidence.
- **N66 Screenshots briefly, as evidence.** "You stayed on the same screen for 29 seconds with just a bit of zooming and no actions"; "show things for much less and support things for longer. So we don't have to have the screenshots on for so long". Rule: a page or table screenshot stays up for one sentence, about 4-8 s. The talk then moves on to pictures that explain. A slow zoom is not an action.
- **N67 Snappy pace.** "We should also speed up the speech. It's just too slow. It's like somebody's reading a bedtime story. It should be snappy". Rule: narration runs at about 150 words a minute (2.5 words per second). Breeze's natural 1.8 words per second needs `atempo` of about 1.35. Gaps between clips are 0.15 s or less.
- **N68 Every sentence is supported; a problem is shown as a problem.** "there was a lot of speech that was not supported"; "When you show something and then say there are problems, you need to either put the word problems in or to illustrate the fact that there are problems and why there are problems and what they struggle with. So do not just stay on a single image." Rule: each clause that adds a claim gets an on-screen action at its words. When the narration says something fails, struggles, is missing or is a problem, the picture shows the failure (the thing trying and failing, a red mark, a blocked path, the missing piece) and, where the narration gives one, the reason. At minimum the word itself appears as a label.
- **N69 Four times the movement.** "There should probably be four times as many graphics in the video as there are right now"; "there needs to be so much more movement in this video than there is at the moment". Rule for `navigate`: a visual event (an arrival, swap, move, highlight or new screen) about every 2-3 s, and never more than 4 s without one while the voice runs. A screen lasts about 6-10 s before the next screen. Measure it: the build tool reports the largest gap per scene, and any gap over 4 s is a defect to fix before rendering. (Reference point: the first v2 render had 114 builds in 7:55, a gap of about 4 s on average and up to 10 s. That is too static.)
- **N70 The storyboard shows movement, not just key images.** "The storyboards would just show the main images ... what I didn't realize when you're showing me the storyboard that these things would be static and that they would just stay there." Rule: the approval storyboard shows every state of every scene (one still per build), with the spoken words and the time at which it arrives, and a timeline strip per scene that marks each event against the narration, so static stretches are visible at a glance. Better still, render a draft MP4 for review (rendering takes minutes) and present it alongside the storyboard.
- **N71 The companion is a learning app, not a long scroll.** "look at https://skillsguide.pages.dev/ for an example of a design - this page is too simplistic" (2026-11-07). Rule: the companion page is a small multi-view app: a home hub (calls to action, one card per experience, the chapter questions with times, progress kept in the browser), the video with a chapter timeline and a searchable transcript that follows playback, a walkthrough that steps through every state of every screen in two panes (figure left; narration with the current cue marked, page reference and "watch this moment" right), the full widgets, the source's key terms as a map linked to the screens that show them, the reading guides, and About. Light and dark themes, keyboard control, works offline from `file://`. `companion.mjs` builds all of it from the outline, frames, cues and `companion.json`; views without data are left out.
- **N72 The companion follows the argument, not the video; guides are folded in, not framed.** "The walkthrough isn't very useful because it doesn't give a nice table, official table of contents, the dots are hard to navigate ... the walkthrough maybe shouldn't replicate the video itself, but maybe the follow-up, the argument"; on the guides: "they're very busy, full of text ... they sort of replicated the paper"; "the research ... is not really research, it's application" (2026-11-07). Rule: the step-through view is organised by the source's argument with a real table of contents (questions, then numbered steps), and the picture builds sit behind thumbnails. Long guides are not embedded; their unique parts (practical application, checklists) become their own views, and reading routes stay optional. Name the practical view for what it does (Apply), and say on the Making view that an agent built everything, with links to the skills.
